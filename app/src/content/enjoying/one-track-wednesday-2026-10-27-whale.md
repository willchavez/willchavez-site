This is the second Duval Timothy track I've written about here, and my second post on [Rhune](https://rhune.co/curator/will/duval-timothy). He has made some kind of peace with himself. I can hear it in the music, and I want it for my own.

Whale seems simple at first. Then it opens, slowly, into a story you know. Not one you've heard before, exactly. One you lived once, and set down somewhere, and forgot you were carrying.
